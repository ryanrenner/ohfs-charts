# Design decisions

Each entry: what was decided, and why. Newest last.

**Two single-axis charts instead of one dual-axis combo chart.**
Dual-axis charts make readers work out which line belongs to which scale.
Splitting price and sales removes that.

**Price shown as a range (low to high) with the average marked.**
With 12 to 35 sales a year, one unusual sale moves the average a lot. The
range gives context. A thinner "dumbbell" range plot was considered and
declined; the tinted range bars were kept.

**Two charts, not three.**
An early mockup had a price line chart plus a range chart. The range chart
with connected averages shows both trend and spread, so the line chart was dropped.

**KPIs, percent changes, and chips are computed, never typed.**
Hand-typed numbers drift from the chart. Generated ones cannot.

**Homes-sold change is a count ("+3 vs. 2024"), not a percent.**
Small counts make percents misleading: 2 to 4 sales would read "+100%".

**Neutral headline, generated lede.**
The original headline "More homes sold, at higher prices" was wrong for real
Sagewood data: sales fell from 35 to 18. The headline is now neutral, and the
lede's direction words come from the data.

**Kicker "Market Trends" in green bold Poppins.**
Ryan's choice. It deviates from the theme's default Playfair italic kicker,
but is scoped to this component only. Green passes contrast on white (about 4.8:1).

**Chip text is `#222` on tint, not green on tint.**
Green on the green tint fails contrast at 12px.

**Thin data shows KPIs only.**
Charts need 2+ years and 5+ average annual sales. Otherwise charts look
empty and imply trends that aren't there.

**One shared snippet, data on GitHub, lookup by page URL.**
371 pages with inline data would need 371 edits per refresh. Now one CSV
rebuild updates every page. Tradeoff: text generated by JavaScript is a
slightly weaker SEO signal than static HTML, which is acceptable because each
page keeps its own written content.

**A script builds the data, not an LLM.**
The export had 5,860 exact duplicate rows in 2022. Aggregation must be exact
and repeatable. An LLM's role is limited to drafting the name mapping, which
a human reviews.

**Property types: single family, townhouse, villa/patio, condo. Under $50K dropped.**
Duplexes and multi-family are excluded as a different market. Sub-$50K sales
are data errors or non-arm's-length transfers.

**City pages don't get the dashboard.**
Bellevue, La Vista, Ralston, and Springfield match an MLS plat of the same
name, but that plat is only the original town, not the whole city.

**Page list comes from the live sitemap, not the CMS spreadsheet.**
`cms_pages_database.xlsx` (March 2026) had 229 of 1,320 pages, likely from a
scrape that stopped early. The sitemap is complete.
